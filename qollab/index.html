After a storm, who fixes what first?

A utility has more broken lines than crews. Here a storm has knocked out eight sites, and every hour a site stays dark costs its urgency. A site is one repair, and its urgency is the repair's priority weight.

Select a site to hand it to crew A; select it again for crew B. Each crew works its most urgent site per hour first. Travel between sites is not part of the model.

Eight sites, two crews: 28 = 256 ways to hand them out. Swapping the names of the crews gives the same night, so there are 128 different plans. Twelve repairs, the size of the test feeder, give 2,048. Best plan means the best of these, with each crew working in the order of the rule. The sites here do not depend on each other, so that order is the best one for each crew.

The classical baseline

Every result is measured against classical methods. The simplest exact one is a full search. Brute force scores each of the 256 plans of this night; a bar's height is how much worse that plan is than the best, and every bar has a twin.

From a plan to qubits

A plan is one choice for each site, crew A or crew B, so one qubit for each site holds a plan. Put two sites on the same crew and the less pressing one waits while the other is repaired. That wait is a link between the two, and its thickness is what the wait costs. Split the two across the crews and the link is cut: nobody waits. A plan is a cut through the links, and the best plan is the one that cuts away the most weight, which is weighted MaxCut.

QAOA in one page: one layer of the circuit, on the same picture

The circuit starts in an equal mix of every plan. The cost layer is one two qubit gate for each link, turned by an angle in proportion to the link's weight, so every plan gets a phase set by the links it pays. The mixer is one rotation on each qubit, which lets the phases of neighbouring plans add or cancel, so cheap plans become likely. Two such layers, a measurement, and each shot is one plan.

What CrewCut does differently

Standard QAOA: 16 qubits

One qubit for each site and crew. A site with both lit, or neither, is not a plan, so a penalty has to push the circuit away from those patterns.

CrewCut QAOA: 8 qubits

One qubit for each site: it says which crew. Every pattern is a plan. No penalty, half the qubits, and one gate for each link: 28 in a layer. The standard form needs every link twice, once for each crew, and a penalty gate for each site: 64. For the twelve repairs of the test feeder that is 12 qubits and 66 gates against 24 and 144.

The one bit form is from scheduling theory (Skutella, 2000). New here: its use for restoration on a feeder, pair weights fitted to scored plans, a thinned cost layer, and the test against standard QAOA and classical search.

Beyond the hackathon

    
Not a claim that QAOA should restore power today. Four groups can use what was measured.

    

      
Research

        A measured price for the penalty: the valid shots of the penalty form get 0.1% to 7.8% of the
          way from random plans to the best plan, those of CrewCut 56% to 73%. Open: which other problems of
          two resources have a pair form without a penalty.
        6 to 10 independent repairs · one and two layers · medians · the penalty form at its best
          penalty · results/standard_qaoa.txt and results/standard_qaoa_10.txt

      
Education

        No quantum background is needed to follow the problem: two crews, repair hours, priorities.
          The experiments run at 6 to 20 qubits on a laptop, and the hosted runs used Qollab's free
          simulators. A syllabus names the source behind each file.

      
Industry

        Utility rules sit 14% and 19% from the best plan, and a classical algorithm closes most of
          that. Near use: a small test workload with a known answer for compilers and machines. Flights
          to runways and surgeries to operating rooms have the same form; nothing was run for those.

      
Community

        Restoration order decides whose power returns first, so a claim about it should be checkable
          by anyone: results tied to result files, classical wins printed beside quantum ones, MIT
          licence.

    

  


  

    
What quantum advantage would take

    
Quantum advantage means beating the best classical method. At this size nothing beats a full search: this page
      scored every plan of its night in under a millisecond, and a classical search of
      every schedule at twelve repairs takes about a millisecond. Its catch is that every added site doubles the
      plans.

Method and scope

    

      
What it is

        CrewCut-QAOA is a quantum circuit that assigns storm repairs to two repair crews. It uses one
          qubit for each repair. This page is a small game with eight repairs. You send the crews out, the
          page scores every plan, and two circuits sample plans for the same storm.

      
Why it matters and who it is for

        After a storm, the crew that each repair goes to decides how long customers stay without power.
          Researchers can use the comparison with standard QAOA. Teachers can use the problem because it needs
          no quantum background. Engineers can use it as a small test job with a known best answer. Classical
          methods still do better at this size, so we do not suggest QAOA for real power restoration
          today.

      
How the quantum part works

        Each repair gets one qubit, and its value picks crew A or crew B. Two repairs on the same crew
          make one of them wait. The circuit has one two qubit gate for every pair of repairs and one rotation
          for every qubit. Each measurement gives one plan, and the best of them is kept. The sections above
          show this.

      
How to run it

        On Qollab, click Run Project and choose the built-in simulator in Select QPU. Every simulator
          there is free. The entries marked "Credits apply" are real quantum computers. Then press Play the demo,
          or click the sites, send the crews, search every plan and run the circuits. On a laptop, install the
          packages in requirements.txt and run these commands.
