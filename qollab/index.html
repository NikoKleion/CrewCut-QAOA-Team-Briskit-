About

What it is

CrewCut-QAOA is a quantum circuit that assigns storm repairs to two repair crews. It uses one qubit for each repair. This page is a small game with eight repairs. You send the crews out, the page scores every plan, and two circuits sample plans for the same storm.

Why it matters and who it is for

After a storm, the crew that each repair goes to decides how long customers stay without power. Researchers can use the comparison with standard QAOA. Teachers can use the problem because it needs no quantum background. Engineers can use it as a small test job with a known best answer. Classical methods still do better at this size, so we do not suggest QAOA for real power restoration today.

How the quantum part works

Each repair gets one qubit, and its value picks crew A or crew B. Two repairs on the same crew make one of them wait. The circuit has one two qubit gate for every pair of repairs and one rotation for every qubit. Each measurement gives one plan, and the best of them is kept. Steps 3 to 5 below show this.

How to run it

On Qollab, click Run Project and choose the built-in simulator in Select QPU. Every simulator there is free. The entries marked "Credits apply" are real quantum computers. Then click the sites, send the crews, search every plan and run the circuits. On a laptop, install the packages in requirements.txt and run these commands.

  • python -m checks.dispatch_game_checks checks the Python code of this page

2 Classical brute force: try every plan

Eight sites, two crews: 28 = 256 ways to hand them out. Swapping the names of the crews gives the same night, so there are 128 different plans and every bar below has a twin. Brute force scores each one; a bar's height is how much worse that plan is than the best.

Best plan means the best of these, with each crew working in the order of the rule. The sites here do not depend on each other, so that order is the best one for each crew.

Brute force is exact, and at this size nothing beats it: this page just did it in - milliseconds. Its catch is that every added site doubles the bars.

3 How CrewCut reads the night: every pair of sites is a link

Put two sites on the same crew and the less pressing one waits while the other is repaired. That wait is a link between the two, and its thickness is what the wait costs. Split the two across the crews and the link is cut: nobody waits. A plan is a cut through the links, and the best plan is the one that cuts away the most weight, which is weighted MaxCut.

One layer of the circuit, on the same picture

The CrewCut circuit has one qubit for each site and one two qubit gate for each link, turned by an angle in proportion to the link's weight. That gives every plan a phase set by the links it pays. One rotation on each qubit then lets the phases of neighbouring plans add or cancel. Two such layers, a measurement, and each shot is one plan.

4 Two ways to write a plan in qubits

Standard QAOA: 16 qubits

One qubit for each site and crew. A site with both lit, or neither, is not a plan, so a penalty has to push the circuit away from those patterns.

CrewCut QAOA: 8 qubits

One qubit for each site: it says which crew. Every pattern is a plan. No penalty, half the qubits, and one gate for each link: 28 in a layer. The standard form needs every link twice, once for each crew, and a penalty gate for each site: 64.

The one bit form is from scheduling theory (Skutella, 2000). New here: its use for restoration on a feeder, pair weights fitted to scored plans, a thinned cost layer, and the test against standard QAOA and classical search.

Education

No quantum background is needed to follow the problem: two crews, repair hours, priorities. The experiments run at 6 to 20 qubits on a laptop, and the hosted runs used Qollab's free simulators. A syllabus names the source behind each file.

Industry

Utility rules sit 14% and 19% from the best plan, and a classical algorithm closes most of that. Near use: a small test workload with a known answer for compilers and machines. Flights to runways and surgeries to operating rooms have the same form; nothing was run for those.

Community

Restoration order decides whose power returns first, so a claim about it should be checkable by anyone: results tied to result files, classical wins printed beside quantum ones, MIT licence.

What this shows and what it does not

  • It shows CrewCut QAOA against standard QAOA on equal terms: same sites, same number of layers, same training. Over 30 nights of this size CrewCut returned a best plan 4 times as often (3.63% of shots against 0.90%) with half the qubits; results/standard_qaoa.txt in the repository.
  • It does not show a quantum speedup. Brute force above is exact and takes a fraction of a millisecond here; training the circuit's angles takes longer than that in simulation.
  • With 256 plans and thousands of shots every sampler sees the best plan, picking at random included. What tells the circuits apart is where the shots pile up.
  • On 16 of the 30 test nights of this size the simple dispatch rule is already a best plan.
  • The figures for this night, the 16 of 30 and the exact shares of best plan shots, are in results/dispatch_game_instance.txt in the repository.
  • Standard QAOA is given the penalty that suits it best on this night, of four tried.
  • Two crews, and no travel time between sites.
  • Everything runs on simulators and noise models, and one night is one night.
