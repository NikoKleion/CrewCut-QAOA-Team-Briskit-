2 Classical brute force: try every plan

Eight sites, two crews: 28 = 256 ways to split them. Brute force scores each one. Every bar below is one plan; its height is how much worse it is than the best.

Brute force is exact, and at this size nothing beats it: this page just did it in - milliseconds. Its catch is that every added site doubles the bars.

3 How Crew-Cut reads the night: every pair of sites is a link

Put two sites on the same crew and the less pressing one waits while the other is repaired. That wait is a link between the two, and its thickness is what the wait costs. Split the two across the crews and the link is cut: nobody waits. A plan is a cut through the links, and the best plan is the one that cuts away the most weight.

One layer of the circuit, on the same picture

The Crew-Cut circuit has one qubit for each site and one two-qubit gate for each link, turned by an angle in proportion to the link's weight. That gives every plan a phase set by the links it pays. One rotation on each qubit then lets the phases of neighbouring plans add or cancel. Two such layers, a measurement, and each shot is one plan.

4 Two ways to write a plan in qubits

Standard QAOA: 16 qubits

One qubit for each site and crew. A site with both lit, or neither, is not a plan, so a penalty has to push the circuit away from those patterns.

Crew-Cut QAOA: 8 qubits

One qubit for each site: it says which crew. Every pattern is a plan. No penalty, half the qubits, and one gate for each link: 28 in a layer. The standard form needs every link twice, once for each crew, and a penalty gate for each site: 64.

The form comes from the scheduling literature. What is measured here is what it buys.

What this shows and what it does not

  • It shows Crew-Cut QAOA against standard QAOA on equal terms: same sites, same number of layers, same training. Over 30 nights of this size Crew-Cut returned a best plan 4 times as often (3.63% of shots against 0.90%) with half the qubits; results/standard_qaoa.txt in the repository.
  • It does not show a quantum speedup. Brute force above is exact and takes a fraction of a millisecond here; training the circuit's angles takes longer than that in simulation.
  • With 256 plans and thousands of shots every sampler sees the best plan, picking at random included. What tells the circuits apart is where the shots pile up.
  • On 16 of the 30 test nights of this size the simple dispatch rule is already a best plan.
  • Standard QAOA is given the penalty that suits it best on this night, of four tried.
  • Everything runs on simulators and noise models, and one night is one night.
