Crew dispatch: you against the circuits

A storm has left eight repairs and you have two crews. Each repair has a length in hours and an urgency: how much each hour without power costs there. Split the repairs between crew A and crew B. Each crew works its most urgent repair per hour first. The score is the urgency weighted hours without power; lower is better.

2. Classical brute force: score every plan

With eight repairs and two crews there are 28 = 256 ways to split them. Swapping the two crews changes nothing, so 128 schedules differ. Brute force scores all of them and keeps the best. This page has just done it, in - milliseconds.

3. The circuits

Two quantum circuits now sample plans for the same eight repairs. Standard QAOA uses one qubit for each repair and crew, 16 in all, and a penalty for plans that put a repair on both crews or on none. Crew-Cut QAOA uses one qubit for each repair, 8 in all: the qubit says which crew. Both have two layers and angles trained beforehand. The backend is the one picked in Select QPU.

Press Run the circuits. Simulators and noise models are free; this game needs no quantum computer.

What this shows and what it does not

  • It shows Crew-Cut QAOA against standard QAOA on equal terms: same repairs, same number of layers, same training. Over 30 instances of this size Crew-Cut returned a best plan 4 times as often (3.63% of shots against 0.90%) with half the qubits; results/standard_qaoa.txt in the repository.
  • It does not show a quantum speedup. Brute force above is exact and takes a fraction of a millisecond here; training the circuit's angles takes longer than that in simulation.
  • With 256 plans and thousands of shots every sampler sees the best plan, random picking included. The numbers that tell the circuits apart are the shares of shots, not whether the best plan turned up.
  • Standard QAOA is given the penalty that suits it best on this instance, of four tried.
  • Everything runs on simulators and noise models, and one instance is one instance.
