Sources for the business figures. Each was opened and the part used was read on 2026-10-11. `inputs.csv` holds the numbers taken from them, `value.py` does the arithmetic and writes `value.csv`. Run as `python docs/money/value.py`.

S1. Executive Office of the President, Economic Benefits of Increasing Electric Grid Resilience to Weather Outages, August 2013, https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2013/08/f2/Grid%20Resiliency%20Report_FINAL.pdf. Page 3: "weather-related outages are estimated to have cost the U.S. economy an inflation-adjusted annual average of $18 billion to $33 billion", for 2003 to 2012, in 2012 dollars. Page 4: "roughly 679 power outages, each affecting at least 50,000 customers, occurred due to weather events" in the same years. Page 10: Superstorm Sandy "knocked out power for 8.5 million customers". Page 21: outage costs due to Sandy in 2012 "are estimated to be $14 to $26 billion". Pages 19 to 21: the cost of an outage is a cost for each customer as a function of how long the customer is out, summed over the customers affected. Inputs: outage_cost_year, sandy_cost, sandy_customers, large_outages, years.

S2. U.S. Energy Information Administration, Today in Energy, 25 January 2024, U.S. electricity customers averaged five and one-half hours of power interruptions in 2022, https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=61303. The U.S. average was "5.6 hours of outages and 1.4 outages per customer"; since 2013 the yearly average has stayed "around two hours after excluding major events". Inputs: hours_out_2022, hours_out_without_major_events.

S3. Sullivan, Schellenberg and Blundell, Updated Value of Service Reliability Estimates for Electric Utility Customers in the United States, Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, LBNL-6941E, January 2015, https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/lbnl-6941e.pdf. Table ES-1, cost per event in 2013 dollars for an interruption of 8 and of 16 hours: residential $17.2 and $32.4; small commercial and industrial $4,690 and $9,055; medium and large commercial and industrial $84,083 and $165,482. Inputs: household, small_business and large_business at 8 and 16 hours.

S4. Amazon Braket pricing, https://aws.amazon.com/braket/pricing/, read 2026-10-11. IonQ Forte: per-task price $0.30000, per-shot price $0.08000. A list price for a machine. No figure in this repository comes from a run on a machine, and the price leaves out the tuning of the circuit, which is done in simulation. Inputs: task_price, shot_price.

S5. Ball and others, Total Delay Impact Study, NEXTOR for the Federal Aviation Administration, 2010, https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/6234. Executive summary: "the total cost of all US air transportation delays in 2007 was $32.9 billion". Input: flight_delay_cost_2007.

S6. Childers and Maggard-Gibbons, Understanding Costs of Care in the Operating Room, JAMA Surgery 153(4), 2018, doi 10.1001/jamasurg.2017.6233. Abstract only: "The mean (SD) cost for 1 minute of OR time across California hospitals was $37.45 ($16.04) in the inpatient setting", fiscal year 2014. Input: operating_room_minute.

Results of this repository. crewcut_100_shots_above_best, random_100_plans_above_best and published_algorithm_above_best are the depth 1 line of results/crew_assignment.txt (means over 20 instances). classical_lead_at_200_plans is the difference between the rows "fitted cost, QAOA depth 2" and "fitted cost, best first" at 200 evaluations in the two crew table of results/budget_experiment.txt (medians over 30 instances; the difference of the means is 0.05 points).

Assumptions. A1: a change of one percent in customer time without power changes the outage cost by one percent. S1 builds its cost from the duration of each customer's outage, and in S3 the cost of an interruption of 16 hours is close to twice that of 8 hours, so this holds roughly for long outages. A2: the rows that turn a result into dollars convert percentage points measured on the IEEE 13 node test feeder at the national rate, to show scale. They do not say that any utility runs these methods, and nothing in this repository shows that the percentages carry over to a real storm. A3: the percentages are relative to the best plan and are applied to the reported cost without rebasing.

The mean large outage divides ten years of the yearly cost by the 679 outages. A few storms carry most of the sum, so a typical outage costs far less than the mean. Dollars are of the year each source gives and are not adjusted for inflation. The operating room day is taken as 8 hours, 480 minutes.
