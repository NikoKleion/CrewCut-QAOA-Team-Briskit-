4,096 bitstrings. 64 are plans.

Standard QAOA spends 12 qubits and a penalty to say “each repair gets exactly one crew.” CrewCut spends 6 qubits and says nothing, because every bitstring already is a plan. Turn the dial below and watch where the probability goes.

On the IEEE 13 node feeder, the best of 100 depth 1 CrewCut shots is 0.32% above the best assignment. The dispatch rules in use today are 14.2% and 19.4% above it. We claim no quantum advantage, and the last panel shows where classical methods win.

Turn the penalty dial

Standard QAOA has to be told that each repair gets exactly one crew. It does that with a penalty, and the penalty has a price. Set it low and shots land on bitstrings that are not plans. Set it high and nearly every shot is a plan, but the circuit has learned the rule instead of the schedule.

CrewCut has nothing to tune. Every cell on its map is a real plan.

Cut the circuit, then add noise

CrewCut has no penalty terms, so every pair term carries cost information and the weakest can be dropped. Without noise that costs a little. Under IonQ’s Aria 1 noise model it helps: half the pair terms keep more of the gain than all of them.

Noise figures come from IonQ’s documented noise models, not from hardware. The Forte 1 model was replaced in September 2025.

What this does not show

  • Quantum advantage. At these sizes classical search reaches the best assignment more often than QAOA, and the paper says so.
  • Results on hardware. Accuracy figures come from an ideal simulator and noise figures from documented noise models. The hosted runs are single runs on one instance.
  • Large problems. The comparison with standard QAOA stops at 10 repairs, 20 qubits for the baseline. Everything else is at 18 repairs or fewer.
  • More than two crews. With three or more, “same crew” is no longer a pair term, and the encoding does not carry over unchanged.
  • That the probability maps are the paper’s data. They come from one seeded 6-repair instance simulated by sim.py, so the exact numbers differ from the paper’s medians over 30 instances.
